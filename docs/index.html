Shadow Rendering under a Rotating Sun

Abstract

High-resolution satellite imagery demands three-dimensional reconstruction methods that deliver both speed and geometric accuracy. Recent adaptations of 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) offer efficient reconstruction, but diverse illumination across multi-date acquisitions and small intersection angles can reduce geometric accuracy.

We present SatSplat, a framework that adapts 2D Gaussian splatting (2DGS) to satellite photogrammetry with online camera adjustment. SatSplat represents scene surfaces using oriented planar Gaussians, refines affine cameras from dense photometric observations, and models time-varying shadows and illumination through geometric shadow mapping and per-camera color correction.

Experiments across DFC2019 and IARPA2016 demonstrate strong geometric accuracy and practical computational efficiency. On the processed DFC2019 benchmark, SatSplat reduces mean absolute error by 11.93% and peak video memory by 31% relative to the previous state of the art, enabling accurate large-scale digital surface modeling.

Method

Affine 2D Gaussian Splatting

Oriented planar Gaussians model the terrain surface with explicit normals. An analytic affine ray-splat intersection and CUDA rasterizer support efficient optimization and geometrically consistent depth rendering.

Online Camera Refinement

A compact delta-affine parameterization refines the initial satellite camera estimates during training. Dense image observations guide the adjustments, improving alignment when the initial camera geometry is imperfect.

Shadow-aware Appearance

Sun directions from acquisition metadata drive geometric shadow mapping. Per-image affine color correction and ambient illumination model accommodate radiometric variation across dates while learning surface albedo.

Geometry-aware Initialization

A coarse stereo DSM and orthophoto initialize the Gaussian positions, colors, and normals. Surface-area-weighted sampling allocates more primitives to steep structures, including building facades.

Code and Reproducibility

The public implementation includes a locked Pixi environment, training and rendering entry points, and preparation scripts for all 13 benchmark scenes. SatSplat exports georeferenced DSMs and orthophotos, as well as trained Gaussian models and registered DSM evaluation metrics.
